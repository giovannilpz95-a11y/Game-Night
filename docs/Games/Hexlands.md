# Hexlands

Back to [[Home]].

An island-settling trading game (the roll-for-resources, build-and-trade family) with our own names: Lumber, Clay, Wool, Grain, Stone, camps, towns, the Bandit, and action cards (Guard, Monument, Road Crew, Harvest, Hoard). The display name is set only in `shared/GameCatalog.luau`.

- Rules: `src/shared/Games/Hexlands.luau`. 2-4 seats, first to `TARGET_POINTS` (8) on their own turn wins; after `MAX_ROUNDS` (40/27/20 rounds for 2/3/4 seats, about the same number of turns each) the most points wins; a tie goes to more towns, then Longest Road, then Biggest Guard (`view.players[s].breakdown` at the end feeds the Final scores list, Monuments revealed).
- Turn: any one action card may be played before rolling (official FAQ, Colonist). Road Crew before the roll returns to the roll afterwards (`state.roadsReturn`). The AFK clock is for the whole turn (`getTurnKey`: per turn, per set-up step, and once for the discards after a 7; catalog `turnSeconds = 90`).
- House rules: points target, Friendly Bandit (players with 2 public points or fewer can't be blocked or robbed: `banditHexes` leaves their hexes out, any other hex if that rules out all of them), safe hand 7/9, balanced dice, Island (`map`: random / spiral / beginner) and Duel rules for 2 players (on by default: 10 points, safe hand 9, Friendly Bandit, balanced dice).
- Board: `src/client/Renderers/Hexlands.luau`. Hexes are drawn as three rotated frames; roads, camps and towns are frames at edge and corner positions taken from the rules module, so the board and the rules always agree. Build buttons pick a mode, then legal spots glow (from `view.options`).
- Rolls: `lastGain` (what the last roll paid each seat, and which hexes) and `rollCounts` (totals 2-12) are public in the view. The 3D board puts a halo under each paying number with what it paid; your screen calls out your own gain and the side panel has Dice stats bars with the expected count as a line.
- Tests: `tests/Hexlands.spec.luau` (geometry, fair maps, setup order, production, 7s and discards, the Bandit, building, bank/harbor rates, trades, action cards, Longest Road, winning, hidden info, bot-vs-bot games for every level and seat count).
- Geometry: 19 pointy-top hexes in axial coordinates, radius 2. `vertices` (54) and `edges` (72) are built once at load with adjacency (`hexes`, `edges`, `near`). Harbors use 9 coast edges spaced evenly so none share a corner.
- Map: `random` shuffles lands and numbers and re-deals (up to 600 times, keeping the fairest) until no 6 or 8 touch, no two equal numbers touch and no 2 touches a 12 (`Hexlands.unfairness` = 0; in practice every map). `spiral` shuffles the lands and lays the classic token order round the island from a random corner (`spiralOrders`). `beginner` is one fixed fair island (`BEGINNER_LANDS`, Dunes in the middle, spiral numbers). The Bandit starts on the Dunes.
- Trades: on your turn, up to `TRADES_PER_TURN` (3) offers, to everyone (`to = 0`, the default in the UI, like Colonist) or one player. Everyone asked answers `accept`, `reject` or `counter` (other terms, written from the offerer's side). A one-player offer trades at once on `accept`. For an open offer the offerer then picks who to trade with (`confirm`, using that player's counter terms if they countered) or calls it off (`cancel`); if everyone says no it closes by itself. Active seats are those still thinking, then the offerer once someone is in. The offer waits `TRADE_SECONDS` (20) through `getTimer`/`onTimeout`; once someone says yes (or only people are left to answer a bot) the rest get `HURRY_SECONDS` (8). When the clock runs out with a yes, unanswered seats count as no and the offerer gets `PICK_SECONDS` (12) to pick (`trade.picking`). A bot offerer isn't active until everyone has answered or the clock ran out, and picks a person's yes over a bot's. Counters must be payable by both sides. `view.trade.answers` is a dense list per seat (-1 not asked, 0 thinking, 1 yes, 2 no, 3 countered).
- Table UI: "You give →" / "← You get" offer builder with Everyone or one player; while an offer is out each asked player has a chip (thinking / ✓ In / ✗ No / ↔ counter terms) and the offerer gets a Trade button on each yes. Answers are big, spaced Accept / Counter / Decline. After a roll your new cards fly from the paying hexes into your hand; the Bandit's hex goes dark; player rows show points as a big number out of the goal and cards in red over the hand limit. Also: "Not trading this turn" on an offer and a "Turn down every trade offer" toggle (both client-side auto-declines), the bank's stock line (red when 3 or fewer), a "7! The Bandit moves" call-out, the News log (last 10, Show all up to `LOG_SIZE` 50), the steal note only for the turn it happened, Harvest picks greyed out when the bank is out, 38 px +/- buttons on phones, and a Final scores list at the end (Table view: Final scores on the bar).
- 3D board (`server/Tables/Boards/Hexlands.luau`): number tokens have their pips; each pier has a floating 3:1 / 2:1 resource sign once the match's harbor kinds are known.
- Hidden info: other players' hands and action cards are counts only, and Monument points stay hidden until the end, even in your own seat row (your view gets them separately as `you.secret`).
- Network: board tables in the view (`owner`, `town`, `road`, `discards`) are dense lists with 0 or false for empty, because sparse number keys arrive as strings through a RemoteEvent. Log entries carry `target` (a seat) and the client adds the name.
- Bot: rule-based. Bots only use public information: Hoard picks from `guessHands` (hand sizes spread by what each player's buildings produce), and offers go to the whole table. Corners are scored by dice pips, resource variety, missing resources and harbors. Build order is town > camp > bank trade toward a build > a 1-for-1 offer to the table when a card short (hard bots follow with a 2-for-1) > road (to keep two camp spots open ahead, or chasing Longest Road) > action card. Guards and Road Crew are played as soon as they can be. The Bandit goes where it hurts the leader most and never on its own hexes. Bots accept trades that bring them closer to a build, and refuse anyone within 2 points of winning.
  - Easy: picks from the top 6 options, makes fewer bank trades and accepts some bad trades.
  - Medium: top 2. Hard: always the best, and chases Longest Road.
- Pacing: bots wait about 0.5 s between moves inside a turn (0.8-1.1 s to roll). Medium and Hard bots reach 8 points in nearly every game (about 15 rounds with 4 seats, 27 with 2), even against a player who never builds; a test checks this. With one human at 10 s a move, a 3-seat match takes roughly 15-20 minutes (checked by the rewards test).
