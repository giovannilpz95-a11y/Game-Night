# Whodunit

Back to [[Home]].

A deduction game for 3-6 seats in the Clue / Cluedo family, with our own mystery ("Who swiped the Golden Trophy?"), suspects, items and rooms. The display name is set only in `shared/GameCatalog.luau`; suspect, item and room names are in `Whodunit.theme` at the top of the rules, so a themed mansion is one table swap.

- Rules: `src/shared/Games/Whodunit.luau`. 21 cards: 6 suspects (ids 1-6), 6 items (7-12), 9 rooms (13-21). One of each goes in the case file, the other 18 are dealt round the table (4 or 5 seats get uneven hands). Seat N plays suspect N. The first seat is random (2023 rules).
- Board: a 3x3 grid of rooms, no dice. Walk to a neighbouring room, or take a secret passage between opposite corners (Kitchen-Library, Observatory-Pool). You may stay put only on your first turn or if someone's suggestion pulled your pawn in (house rule `stay`: always, like the 2023 rules).
- Turn phases: `move` -> `suggest` (room = where you are; the named pawn and item move in) -> `show` (first seat clockwise holding a match picks one to show, always, even with one match, so the table can't tell how many they hold) -> `after` (end turn). Suggesting is optional: `end` in the `suggest` step skips it. Accuse any time on your turn except while a card is being shown. Wrong = out, but you still show cards. Everyone out = draw. 300 turns = draw (safety valve).
- Hidden info: `getView` sends your own hand, the card in a suggestion only to the asker and the shower, and the case file only once the game is over. Everyone sees who showed and who couldn't. `notes` is the auto notepad (your cards + cards shown to you), sent as a list so it survives the remote.
- Table: `src/client/Renderers/Whodunit.luau`. Mansion map (rooms you can walk to glow; tap to move; pawns pop when they move), action panel (suggest chips, end turn, and a separate red Accuse bar pinned at the bottom), and Detective notes / Case log tabs.
- Detective notes are a grid like the paper sheet: a row per card, a column per other player. The game fills in what it knows (✓ for whoever showed you a card, ✗ for everyone else then, ✗ for anyone who couldn't disprove a suggestion naming it, from the public `view.lacks`); tap any other box to cycle · ✗ ? ✓. Names get crossed off once you know who holds them. Marks are client-only, kept per match for the session (a re-created table keeps them). The "#" switch stamps the latest suggestion number instead of cycling marks; "Smart notes" (off by default) fills in every card whose holder can be worked out (`view.deduced`, hard deduction from what you may see) and flags case-file cards. Touch screens get 36 px rows.
- Suggestion go-round: after a suggestion each seat asked lights up in turn (0.55 s apart) with "No card" or "Shows a card" (`view.logCount` keys it). The player who must show gets a pop-up with only the matching cards, big.
- Compact layout (phones, or a board area under 640x420): map left, actions right, notes open as a full sheet from the Notes button.
- A new suggestion shows a short call-out for everyone ("🔎 Ava suggests ...").
- End: the case file's three cards come out one at a time, then every hand is listed with how many case-file cards each player had worked out (`view.hands`, `view.solvedCount`). A match that ends early (end for all, everyone left) shows "This game has ended." instead of turn prompts.
- Table view: room floors on the 3D board carry `TapId` (the room) and `CanQuery`; tapping one walks there (`renderer.tapTable`). Room names are nameplates on each room's floor (a floating label drifted off its room in perspective).
- A card shown to you flips face up in the middle of your screen (`HudKit.revealCard`).
- Bots: `Whodunit.deduce(state, seat, level)` tracks which holder (each seat or the case file) could have each card, using only what that seat may know. Easy: own hand and cards shown to it. Medium: also who couldn't disprove. Hard: also hand sizes and "seat X showed someone one of these three". Bots accuse only when each category has one candidate left, so they never accuse wrong. They walk toward unsolved rooms and name unsolved suspects and items; hard bots name their own cards once a category is solved. When showing, medium and hard bots re-show a card the asker has already seen. Hard bots suggest to test one unknown card at a time (the other two from their own hand when they can). Easy bots wander at random a quarter of the time. The AFK clock (`chooseAfkMove`) walks, skips the suggestion and ends the turn; it never accuses.
- Pacing: bots make a minimum number of suggestions before accusing even when sure (`minSuggestionsToAccuse`: easy 3, medium 5, hard 4), so a human table gets a real game. Bot-only games: about 15-22 turns on hard, 17-36 on medium, 40-100 on easy. Catalog: 70 s for a whole turn (`getTurnKey`; showing a card gets its own clock), `typicalMinutes = 10`, `minMinutes = 1`, `minMoves = 8`, pays Whodunit Coins.
- Tests: `tests/Whodunit.spec.luau` (deal, movement, disproving order, privacy, accusations, deduction, bot games at every level and table size).
- Not yet: 3D mansion mode, pawn and item models (`Assets.Whodunit`), themed mansions, dice-movement variant.
- Accusations open the case file on screen: the envelope flap lifts, the cards flip and a SOLVED!/WRONG! stamp lands.
